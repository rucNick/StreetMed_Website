Ongoing Research Projects

Street Medicine at Pitt is committed to advancing the field through innovative engineering solutions and interprofessional education. Our ongoing projects focus on improving the quality of life for people experiencing homelessness while training the next generation of healthcare professionals.

Active Research

Decreasing Effects of Prolonged Noise Exposure in Homeless Encampments

Project Team: Ella Jones, Amechi Oruwari, Sean Springer, Colin Henchy
Faculty Advisor: Dr. Lee Fisher, Street Medicine at Pitt

University of Pittsburgh Swanson School of Engineering's senior design class worked with Street Medicine at Pitt to create a project that aims to provide aid to the residents of a Pittsburgh-area homeless encampment located alongside an interstate. Sound levels along high-traffic areas can reach as high as 95 dB, with hearing damage occurring at prolonged exposure of 85 dB or above. The team developed a 3 ft x 6 ft sound panel using Helmholtz resonators built into the interior of the panel to decrease ambient sound below 85 dB, targeting low frequencies (550, 1000, and 1500 Hz) prevalent in traffic noise.

Goal: Reduce sound below 85 dB
Status: Prototype Completed

Key Achievements

  • Successfully lowered sound levels above 85 dB to below threshold
  • Achieved over 20 dB reduction when the barrier was in place
  • Lightweight, durable, and user-friendly design
  • Low-cost solution under $300
  • Size: approximately 3 ft x 6 ft panel
Active Research

Interprofessional Education Experiences in Homeless Healthcare

Research Team: Rosemary Sabatino MS, Jatin Singh NRP, Max Hurwitz DO
Faculty Leads: Anna Marie White MD, Caroline Passerrello EdD, RDN, LDN

This innovative study examines experiential learning for paramedic and dietitian-nutritionist students through Street Medicine at Pitt (SMAP) rounds. Interprofessional education (IPE) remains a cornerstone of clinical education for all healthcare professionals aiming for high quality patient care. The research evaluates how IPE transforms students' understanding of homeless healthcare delivery, clinical collaboration, and patient-centered care. Two School of Health and Rehabilitation Sciences (SHRS) programs—Emergency Medicine (EM) and the Dietitian-Nutritionist Program (DNP)—participated in street medicine rounds during the 2023-2024 academic year.

27 Students (12 EM + 15 DNP)
24+ Health Disciplines Involved

Key Findings

  • 81% of participants agreed SMAP is an effective community-health clinical opportunity
  • Significant increase in understanding interprofessional collaboration
  • Enhanced knowledge of the courses taken by and training requirements of other health professionals
  • Improved patient/client satisfaction when care is delivered by an interprofessional team
  • Healthcare costs are reduced when patients/clients are treated by an interprofessional team

Research Impact & Future Directions

Next Steps

Noise Reduction Project

Engineering Design Team

Future improvements include: running more tests to limit data anomalies, designing full-length fence panels to target a larger array of frequencies, determining how the product would be mass produced and assembled, and designing panels so they can be attached to any size fence or stand alone while still ensuring sound dampening.

Impact

Interprofessional Education Program

SHRS Programs Collaboration

Participants believed that working with students from different health disciplines positively supplements their education, enhances their ability to work with an interprofessional team, and increases their knowledge of other team members' roles. This model successfully increased the diversity and sustainability of IPE experiences by transforming extra-curricular street medicine volunteering into curriculum-based clinical opportunities.
